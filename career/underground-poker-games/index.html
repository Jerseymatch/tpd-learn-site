Should You Deal Underground Poker Games?

A lot of dealers get their start in underground games, and a lot of people ask me if they should. Here's my honest answer: the risk you take, the habits casinos can spot in seconds, and how to make the move to a real poker room.

By Marc Shumsker · Updated November 19, 2026 · 7 min read

People ask me two versions of the same question. Should I start out dealing in an underground game? And: I've been dealing underground for years, so why isn't that getting me a casino job?

I'll answer both. The short version is that I can't support anything illegal. And what counts at a casino audition is casino mechanics and procedures, where habits nobody corrected can work against you. The good news is that you can learn that.

The risk: it's your name on the line

Whether a dealer in an illegal game can get charged depends on the laws where you are. What I will tell you is that if you take part in something illegal, you're putting yourself at risk. I'm not a lawyer, and this isn't legal advice. If you have a question about your own situation, ask a lawyer where you live.

Keep the bigger goal in mind, too. A casino job usually comes with a gaming license and a background check, which poker dealer requirements explains.

What casinos see in underground dealers

In an underground game, nobody corrects you. You build habits, and nobody ever tells you they're wrong. A veteran dealer I interviewed, who started in New York underground games, said she was taught by whoever happened to be around. There was no long learning curve either: new dealers started on the lower games, and players could get a dealer pulled in the middle of a hand, with other dealers waiting to take the seat.

A casino is different. Casinos need procedures that surveillance can follow. That's what an audition is checking. I've run more than 1,000 live dealer auditions, and I can tell almost instantly when someone learned to deal underground. Cutting chips with two hands instead of one is a giveaway. What poker rooms look for covers the rest of what they're watching.

So years in a home or underground game won't get you through an audition on their own, and habits that nobody corrected can actually work against you.

The underground habits I see most

Here are habits I've corrected that come from home and underground games:

  • Cutting chips with two hands. A casino dealer cuts with one. See how to cut chips.
  • Passing chips from hand to hand. Say a player tosses you a tip that lands way over on your left. A lot of dealers, especially ones who started underground, reach for it and pass it from one hand to the other. Many never learn not to, and it's so hard to undo later. How to handle chips shows the right way.
  • A bent index finger on the shuffle. Anyone who has played a lot of home-game poker tends to bend the index finger on top of the deck during the riffle, because that's what they've seen other people do. Keep it flat. You get more control and much less tension in your hands. How to shuffle cards walks through the grip.
  • Holding the deck after the wash. In a home game people pick up the cards and just hold them. In a casino, as soon as you're done washing, you put the deck down flat and go straight into your shuffle. There's no time where you're holding it.

What I correct in class

When a student has played a lot of home games, they almost always have the bent index finger on the riffle. It's not a talent problem. It's an instinct from watching other people shuffle. We flatten the finger and put the thumbs on the corners so they take over control. The same goes for the deck that hangs in the air after the wash: put it down and go.

Why casinos care so much: game protection

Underground games are where the things casino procedure is designed to stop are easiest to pull off. On my channel, magicians have shown how it works. In a game with no cut card, the worst kind of underground game, a cheat can secretly undo the cut and deal a known card from the bottom. In games where whoever has the button deals, a card can be controlled into a hand. And in a game where the dealer also plays, a skilled cheat can hold out cards while gathering the previous board. One magician held out two aces right in front of me. I was watching for it, and I maybe saw a flash.

That's why casinos insist on cut cards and a shuffle procedure that's done the same way every time. It's not fussiness. It's protecting the game. Game protection and surveillance explain what you'll be expected to do.

Texas, where this question comes up most

Texas has a lot of underground games and a home-game feel to its poker. When I got to Texas in 2021, there were no in-person dealer schools, and a lot of the dealers I met had learned from my videos. Many Texas applicants carry bad mechanical habits from underground games, and the Dallas rooms often reject those habits.

You can see the underground roots in the games, too. Underground rooms raked heavily, so they wanted huge pots and allowed things like match the stack, and players came to expect that in the legal rooms.

The good rooms hold a casino standard on purpose. Texas Card House hires real casino people, and it feels like casino poker. (So you know: Texas Card House partners with my Remote Dealer School, and I still deal there.) Is a room like that strict on dealers? A good room has to be. There are write-ups for not following the procedure you were trained on, and it makes a better room. More on that room in working at Texas Card House.

It's a little easier to get hired in Texas, because there are fewer professional dealers, but the rooms still have a standard. If you put eight weeks into learning the casino way, you'd have an edge over a lot of the people applying.

How to move from underground games to a casino job

  1. Relearn the mechanics the casino way. That means your shuffle, pitch and chip handling. Habits nobody corrected are the first thing an auditioner notices.
  2. Learn the procedures, not just the game. Knowing how to play isn't the same as knowing casino procedure for every step of a hand. Start with how to deal a hand of Texas hold'em.
  3. Use your connections. If you know people who've made the move from underground games to a casino, talk to them.
  4. Time your application. Rooms tend to hire when they need dealers, like around a tournament series or an opening. How to get a poker dealer job covers where and when to apply.
  5. Prepare for the audition specifically. How to pass a poker dealer audition lays out what's tested.

Expect the casino schedule to feel different, too. A viewer once told me they'd deal 10 to 12 hours at one underground table with one break. That's doable, but it's a lot. In a casino you rotate through tables with regular breaks, which you can read about in a day in the life of a poker dealer.

How to practice the casino way

The fix for underground habits is repetition with the right technique, on camera, where you can see what you're really doing. Film yourself, compare it with the lesson videos and fix one thing at a time. How to practice dealing at home has the full routine.

Common questions about underground poker games

Is it illegal to deal in an underground poker game?

It depends on the laws where you live. Whether a dealer can be charged varies, but taking part in an illegal game puts you at risk.

Does underground dealing experience count for a casino job?

Not on its own. Casinos judge you on casino mechanics and procedures, and habits nobody corrected can hurt you at the audition.

Can an underground dealer pass a casino audition?

Yes, if they relearn the mechanics the casino way. Experienced auditioners can spot underground habits quickly, so fix them before you go in.

How do I go from dealing home games to working in a casino?

Relearn your shuffle, pitch and chip handling the casino way, learn the procedures, use people who've made the move, apply when rooms are hiring and prepare for the audition.

Do Texas poker rooms hire dealers who learned underground?

They can, but Dallas rooms often reject the habits people learned in underground games. Rooms that hold a casino standard want casino mechanics.

Are underground poker games safe from cheating?

Games with no cut card, or where the dealer or whoever has the button also plays, are where these cheating moves are a real danger. That's exactly what casino procedures are built to prevent.
