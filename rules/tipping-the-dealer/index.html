How Much Should You Tip a Poker Dealer?

There's no required amount, and tipping is always your decision. Here's what's normal in a live poker room, when and how to tip, what happens to the chip after you hand it over, and what a good dealer will never do about it.

By Marc Shumsker · Updated November 20, 2026 · 8 min read

Players ask me this all the time: how much am I supposed to tip the dealer? I've dealt since 2007, been a poker supervisor, and I still pick up dealing shifts, so I'll give you the honest answer from the dealer's side of the table.

The short version is that there's no required amount, and it's 100% your decision. But there are some norms, a right and wrong way to hand a tip over, and a few things about where that chip goes that most players never think about.

How much to tip in a cash game

There's no required amount. A dollar on any pot that reaches the flop is appreciated. On a big pot, players usually tip more. That's it.

The unwritten rule is that you tip when you win a pot. If you don't, a professional dealer isn't going to say anything, and nobody on staff should. Dealers even have a name for players who don't tip: stiffs. The good ones don't take it personally.

A little dealer slang while we're here: a $5 red chip given as a tip is a "red bird." A $25 green chip is a green bird, and a $100 black chip is a black bird, which is rare. Nobody says white bird.

Should you tip when you're losing?

The unwritten rule is to tip when you win a pot, so no, it isn't expected when you're down for the session. Tipping is 100% your decision. For what it's worth, when I play, I tip even when I'm down. That's just me.

How to hand the dealer a tip

This part matters more than the amount, because the wrong way slows the whole game down.

  • Never put the tip on top of your cards. Your cards and your tip go to two different places. The dealer has to stop, pull the chip off, muck the cards, and then deal with the tip. That breaks their rhythm, and it also risks flipping a card face up, because a card is light and easy to lift. It's one of my biggest pet peeves. I've explained it to players at my table, and they've told me they'll stop.
  • Tip after the pot is pushed. Let the dealer push you the pot, then toss the chip over. That's the cleanest way.
  • Make it obvious that it's a tip. Toss it clearly to the dealer and say something like "for you." Be careful with a chip that lands in front of you when you're facing a bet, especially an all-in. A chip put forward facing an all-in is normally treated as a call. If a player says right away that it was meant as a tip, the dealer calls the floor, and the floor decides. Make it clear so it never comes to that.
  • A tip during a hand is fine in a cash game. Sometimes a player tips before the flop because they have a feeling about the hand. The dealer will thank you and drop it right away.

What happens to your tip

A lot of players wonder whether the dealer actually gets the chip. Here's how it works in a real poker room.

  • Tap and drop. The dealer takes the chip with their fingertips, taps it on the table or the corner of the rack, and drops it into a locked tip box. The tap is a cue for surveillance that a tip is going in. Everything stays in full view of the cameras.
  • The box travels with the dealer. At the end of the shift, a cashier gives the dealer the key. The dealer dumps and racks the chips under surveillance, and the cashier counts and verifies them.
  • It's watched. Only what's in the box gets paid out. If a dealer's tips ever come out far outside the normal range, surveillance looks into it. That's why stealing tips doesn't work.

Do poker dealers keep their own tips?

In the US, almost all do now. I'd say about 99% of poker rooms are keep-your-own. Some rooms take a small tip-out, often around 10%, that goes to the supervisors so their pay can compete with the dealers'. A few casinos still make poker dealers pool tips with table games, but that's much less common than it used to be. My first poker job, at a small casino in Washington State, pooled poker and table games tips for my first two years.

I'm against pooling in poker. When every dealer keeps their own, every dealer has a reason to be good and deal faster. The room drops more rake, and the players get better games.

How dealers handle your tip

  • Say thank you, every single time. Poker rooms enforce it. Skipping the thank-you gives a reluctant tipper a reason to stop.
  • Never pass a tip from hand to hand, and never tap the table when you drop the rake. The tap means "tip" to surveillance.
  • Make change to keep the rake up to date, even for small amounts. It's part of the job, and it also puts white chips in the pot, which makes it more likely you get tipped. (See making change.)

Does tipping get you better treatment?

Dealers are supposed to be impartial, and in the game itself they have to be. Every hand gets dealt the same way, and the rules are the same for every player at the table. Dealers are human, though, and most of them do root a little for the players who tip. That's natural. A good dealer keeps the same attitude with the players who don't, and sometimes those players tip later.

Here's what tipping doesn't buy:

  • No leeway on the rules. Regular games run better with consistent standards. It can be hard for a dealer to enforce a rule on a big tipper, and that's exactly why a dealer can tap out and tell the supervisor. A room that never deals with its money players isn't run properly.
  • No protection for a careless hand. I've seen dealers snag an unprotected hand from a player they considered a bad tipper. That's wrong, and it's a game protection problem, but it's also a reason to always protect your cards. Here's what kills a hand.

Playing at a good pace and handing your tip over the right way helps the whole table. For a dealer, every hand is another chance to get tipped, so a fast game is good for everybody.

What a professional dealer will never do

  • Ask or hint for a tip. No hard hustle and no soft hustle. It pressures players, and a dealer can get fired for it.
  • Call you out for how much you tipped, or for not tipping. That's extremely unprofessional for anyone on staff. Sometimes other players at the table will tell a winner to tip more after a lucky pot. A dealer can never do that.
  • Refuse a tip. Even from a player who's been difficult. The dealer takes it and says thank you.

And the thank-you should be sincere but cool. Table games dealers are often taught to thank a player out loud. In poker, over-thanking actually discourages tips.

If a dealer ever yells at a table for not tipping, that's supremely unprofessional, and it's not how tipping works.

Tipping in poker tournaments

Tournaments work differently. Most tournaments now include a staffing fee in the entry that goes to the dealers, split by how many downs each dealer dealt. Tips from the winners are extra, and big tournament tips usually go into the pool rather than to one dealer.

How much should a tournament winner tip? Some people say 10%, but that's far above what pros usually give. A few percent is more typical. Like everything else here, it's your call.

Because nobody's tipping hand by hand in a tournament, a good tournament dealer just stays quiet and cranks out hands. Dealing the WSOP and the circuit has more on how that side works.

Do you tip the poker dealer when you lose?

It isn't expected. Players usually tip when they win a pot. Some tip anyway.

Is it rude to put your tip on your cards?

Any tip is appreciated, but it slows the game down and risks flipping a card. Hand the tip over separately, after the pot's pushed.
