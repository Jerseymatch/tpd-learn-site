Poker Dealer Terms: A Glossary of Poker Room Lingo

Your first week in a poker room, people will say things like "you're on the push," "grab a fill" and "sit the dead spread" like you already know what they mean. Here's the lingo, in plain English, with a link to the full guide for each one.

By Marc Shumsker · Updated November 20, 2026 · 12 min read

Every poker room has its own vocabulary, and nobody stops to define it. A supervisor tells you you're on a brush, a dealer asks if you've got the push, a player tosses you a red bird. This glossary covers the words you'll hear as a dealer: the job itself, the deck, the chips and the bank, the betting and the showdown.

Each term is short on purpose. Where there's a full guide, the term links to it.

The job: rotation, breaks and the people

The box

The dealer's seat. The dealer "in the box" is the one dealing the table right now. See a day in the life of a poker dealer.

Down

Your time at one table, usually 30 minutes (sometimes 20 or 40). See the rotation: downs and breaks.

String

The set of tables you rotate through, one down after another. A room might put you on table 2, then 6, then 9. It's supposed to be balanced by game and by the tables that tip, so it's fair. As a supervisor, I never played favorites with it.

The push

The dealer who comes to take over your table when your down is up. The dealer coming to take your table is "pushing" you. (To "push the pot" is different: that's delivering the pot to the winner.) See tapping in, tapping out and the push.

Tap in, tap out

Taking over a table (tapping in) or being relieved (tapping out). Typically you tap in on the side opposite the tip box, and rooms differ on which side the tip box is on.

Brush

Back in the day, someone went around brushing off the tables, and that person also seated players. The name stuck. The brush is the first person a new player talks to: they sign players up and seat them. A "brush down" is when a busy room puts you on that job in the rotation instead of a break, for 15 to 20 minutes of lights, fills and player needs. Some dealers like the extra money. I'd rather take the break.

Dead spread

A dealer sitting at an empty table with a bank, ready to open a game the moment there are players. See what a dead spread is.

Early out (EO)

Leaving before the end of your shift. Some rooms keep an EO list: you tell the supervisors you'd like to leave as early as possible, and when it's time to cut dealers, they go down the list.

The floor

The floor supervisor. The floor is responsible for the whole room, and the floor makes the rulings. A dealer explains the rules; when there's a decision to make, you call the floor. See when a dealer should call the floor.

Dual rate

A dealer who also works as a floor supervisor: you deal some days and floor others. See dealer or floor?

Chip runner

The person who does fills for dealers whose banks are running low, gets chips for players, cashes players out, collects time in rooms that charge by the hour, and helps with anything else the room needs. It's one of the best ways into a poker room. See from chip runner to dealer.

Stiff

A player who doesn't tip.

Red bird (green bird, black bird)

Dealer slang for a chip given as a tip. A $5 red chip is a red bird, a $25 green is a green bird, and a $100 black is a black bird, which is rare. Nobody says white bird. See tipping the dealer.

The cards and the deck

Setup

Two decks with different-colored backs, one dark and one light, because each poker table uses two decks. See what a setup is.

Suited deck

A deck in order, suit by suit. When people in a poker room say "new deck," they usually mean a suited one. You verify every card, front and back, before it goes into play. See checking a suited deck.

The well

Where the second deck waits while you deal with the first, and where buy-in cash goes. Some people say "the well" for the whole bank.

Wash (full wash, mini wash)

Mixing the cards face down on the felt before you riffle. The full wash, about seven seconds, is for your first hand at a table and for a new suited deck. The mini wash, two to three seconds, is between every other hand. See how to shuffle cards.

Box (in the shuffle)

The step in the shuffle where you move packets of cards from the top of the deck to the bottom, without ever lifting the cards up. The sequence is wash, square, riffle, riffle, box, riffle, cut.

Cut card

The card that goes on the bottom of the deck after the cut. Casinos insist on cut cards: in a game with no cut card, a cheat can secretly undo the cut and deal a known card from the bottom. For practice, you can use a joker as a cut card, but it presents poorly.

Pitch

Dealing the cards to each player. See how to pitch cards.

Burn card

The card you take off the top before the flop, turn and river. Its job is to protect the top card when there's action, so nobody can see what's coming. See how to bring the flop, turn and river.

Stub

The cards left after a hand is dealt. Where there's no shuffle machine, you count it during your down. See counting the stub.

Muck

The pile of folded cards, and the verb for throwing your hand away. A player who mucks at the showdown has a dead hand. See the showdown and protecting the muck.

Exposed card

Any card that flashes or lands face up when it isn't supposed to. See exposed cards.

Box card

A card that's face up inside the deck. It isn't an exposed card: it goes straight to the muck as if it never existed, and in most rooms you call a supervisor (some want to kill it themselves). Two box cards is a misdeal. See misdeals.

Premature card

A flop, turn or river the dealer brings before the betting round is complete. It's always a floor call. See premature cards.

Chips, the bank and the money

Cheque, cheque change

In casinos, chips are also called cheques. Cheque change is exchanging a player's chips for the same amount in different denominations. See buy-ins, cheque change and color-ups.

Color up

Exchanging smaller chips for fewer, larger ones, like reds for greens. Not every room lets dealers do it, and you need room in your bank.

Bank

The chips in front of the dealer, used for buy-ins and for making change. See working the poker bank.

Impress

The fixed amount a bank is supposed to hold. In poker, the bank is an impress: a set amount that stays the same, so you can count it over the other dealer's shoulder when you tap in. Some rooms "impress" a bank back to its starting stacks when a table closes.

Fill

Chips brought to the table to restock a dealer's bank. The point of the bank is that the game can run as long as possible without stopping, so a fill should bring the bank back to where it lasts.

20-stack and spacer

A full stack of chips is 20. Every full stack in the bank gets capped with a clear spacer, so if there's no spacer on top, the next dealer can assume it's not a full stack.

Splash

Spreading chips out on the felt so anyone, including surveillance, can read the amount. When a short stack is incomplete, keep it splashed. See how to cut chips and how to count down bets.

Clear your hands

Quickly showing the camera your palm and the back of your hand, after you touch chips, cash or the deck. See clear your hands.

Rake

The casino's money, taken out of the pot. In a cash game, it's on you, every hand. See rake, promo drop and tips.

Jackpot drop, promo drop

Money taken from the pot, separate from the rake, that the room gives back to players: a bad beat jackpot that builds up and pays out under a rare circumstance, or promotions like high hands. See bad beat jackpots.

Time, time rake

In larger games, many rooms charge players for their time instead of raking every pot.

The game: blinds, betting and pots

Button, dead button

The button marks the dealer position for the hand. If the player who was the small blind busts or leaves, the button still goes to that empty seat. That's a dead button. See straddles, missed blinds and the button.

Buying the button

A new player sitting between the button and the small blind can, in most rooms, post and play right away instead of waiting.

Straddle

A voluntary blind, only in cash games.

2-5-5

Usually a third blind. Sometimes it means a $5 big blind ante for dead money. 2-5-10 would be a forced straddle, or 2/5 with a $10 button straddle.

Kill pot

In some limit games, when one player wins a pot over a set amount, the next hand is played at higher stakes. See kill pots.

Match the stack

A cash game where the table opens with a max buy-in, and after that anyone can buy in up to the biggest stack at the table. You don't count down the whole big stack; you eyeball it.

Must-move game

The first table is the main game. A second table feeds it, from a list in arrival order, moving players as main-game seats open, and a third can feed the second. It protects players from having their game break with no seats anywhere else.

Verbal declaration

A player saying their action out loud. A verbal action is binding over a physical one. See verbal declarations.

String bet

Going back to your stack for more chips without saying "raise" first. A bet is one forward motion. "I call and raise" is a verbal string bet. See the technical rules of betting.

All in, all-in for less

All in means a player has put their whole stack in. An all-in for less than a full raise never changes the raise amount, and the player is only entitled to the money they were able to match. See all-in rules.

Main pot, side pot

The main pot is the pot every player in the hand has put an equal amount into. Each side pot is the next layer up, for only the players who put in more. See how to build side pots.

The trail

In the pot-limit formula (three times the last bet, plus the trail, plus the pot), the trail is all the active bets in the same betting round that are in front of players who aren't in action. See dealing Omaha.

Bomb pot

A separate hand within the same game: everyone who's playing puts out an ante, there's no preflop action, and you bring two boards instead of one. See bomb pots.

Run it twice

When the players who are all in agree to deal the rest of the board two times instead of once. See running it twice.

Angle shooting

Gaining an edge outside the rules. For example, a dealer doesn't bring the bets in before the turn, and a player quietly takes back their 50. That's why you bring the bets in first. See game protection.

Substantial action

How poker handles situations where the proper ruling no longer has an opportunity to be made. Once there's been substantial action, a misdeal can't be called. See misdeals.

The showdown

Cards speak

The only thing that entitles a player to win is their cards. See cards speak and one player per hand.

One player per hand

Each player plays completely on their own.

Tabled hand

A hand turned face up on the table at the showdown. Once it's tabled, it's tabled, and the cards speak. A player who holds their cards up to show them and then mucks never tabled their hand. See the showdown.

Dead hand

One player's cards are killed and the hand plays on without them. See dead hands.

Slow roll

Making an opponent think they've won, then showing the winner. Cards speak, so a slow roll doesn't change who wins. See showdown disputes.

Quartered

Winning a quarter of the pot, usually in a high-low game. See split pots and quartering.

Game names

Big O, Congress

Big O is five-card Omaha. In Texas, Big O is called Congress. I don't know for sure why. One possible origin is a "Big O" on Congress Avenue in Austin. See dealing Omaha.

Razz

Seven card stud for the low hand. See stud, draw and mixed games.

Badugi

A four-card, limit triple-draw game, eight players at most, with no ante. Every four-card badugi beats every three-card hand.

Pineapple

Players get three hole cards and discard one, either before the flop or after it, depending on the version. Rare games often don't have universal rules, so ask which version the room plays.

Lingo I don't want you using

Some words you'll hear at the table are words I'd rather you never say:

  • "Down" on folds, or "player down."
  • "Good luck" on an all-in. Wishing luck is a table-games habit. Never in poker. (Why is in poker vs table games.)

What I like instead is clear action with a bit of personality, like "raise to 20," as long as it never affects the game. More on what to say is in how to deal a hand of Texas hold'em and talking to players.

Common questions about poker dealer terms

What does "down" mean for a poker dealer?

A down is your time at one table, usually 30 minutes. Dealers rotate from table to table, one down at a time.

What does "the push" mean in a poker room?

The push is the dealer who comes to take over your table when your down is up.

What is a brush in poker?

The person who signs players up and seats them, usually the first person a new player talks to. A "brush down" is time in the rotation spent doing that job instead of dealing.

What is a red bird in poker?

Dealer slang for a $5 red chip given as a tip. A $25 green chip is a green bird and a $100 black chip is a black bird.

What does 2-5-5 mean in poker?

Usually a 2/5 game with a third blind of $5. Sometimes it means a $5 big blind ante.

What is the impress in poker?

The fixed amount a dealer's bank is supposed to hold, so any dealer can count it and know whether it's right.
