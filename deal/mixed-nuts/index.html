Mixed Nuts: Free Poker Hand-Reading Practice

Reading the winning hand fast and right is one of the most important things a dealer does. Mixed Nuts is the best practice I know for it, and it was built by one of our own.

By Marc Shumsker · Updated November 23, 2026 · 3 min read

At showdown, the whole table is waiting on you to read the hands and push the pot to the right person. Reading the winning hand quickly and correctly is one of the most important skills a dealer has, and the only way to get fast at it is reps.

The best tool I know for those reps is Mixed Nuts. It's a free hand-reading practice game, and every one of my Remote Dealer School students uses it.

Built by Michael Garber

Mixed Nuts was created by Michael Garber: a True Poker Dealer student, a working dealer, and a former programmer from Minnesota who released his games as RiffRaff Games. He built it to help dealers practice reading hands, and he made it free.

It spread fast. People at Texas Card House in Dallas passed it around to go from chip runner to dealer, and I've had all my Remote School students using it ever since.

Michael is brilliant, and he's been generous with what he built. I'm hosting Mixed Nuts here so dealers can keep using it now that it's out of the app stores, with his name on it where it belongs. Thank you, Michael.

Mixed Nuts trains your eyes. In class, I put you on a live table and watch you read the hand and push the pot. In Remote Dealer School you deal on camera and I correct you live.

How to practice with it

Mixed Nuts shows you practice boards with several hands, and you pick the winning hand. It shows you your mistakes, so you learn the hand you misread instead of just getting a wrong answer.

  • Start with hold'em. Get to where you can read every hand with zero errors before you worry about speed.
  • Then work on speed. Time yourself on 10 hands. Accuracy first, always. A fast wrong read is worse than a slow right one.
  • Move to the mixed games. It covers the flop games (hold'em, Omaha 8, Big O 8), the stud games (7-stud 8 and razz) and the draw games (2-7 triple draw, Badugi and Baducey). Badugi is hard, because you have to count suits.

For a benchmark, here are my own times for 10 hands with zero errors: hold'em 1:08.97, 2-7 triple draw 1:48.80, Omaha 8 2:39.52, Badugi 3:12.79 and Big O 3:17.72.

The app is harder than a real table. At a real table you aren't reading five hands every time, and players protect their own hands. So if Mixed Nuts feels hard, that's the point. If you can read it here, you can read it at the table.

Where it fits in your practice

Use Mixed Nuts alongside the rest of your home practice. Learn the hand rankings and how I read hands at showdown first, then use Mixed Nuts to build speed. For split pots, learn the procedure on the table, because the game won't teach you how to push the chips.
