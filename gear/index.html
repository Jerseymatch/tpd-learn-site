What You Need to Practice Dealing Poker at Home

The exact setup I recommend to every student in my course. You don't need much, and you don't need to spend a lot, but a couple of these items make a bigger difference than people expect.

By Marc Shumsker · Updated November 14, 2026 · 6 min read

When students start my course, the first thing they ask is what they need to buy. Here's the list I give them. These are the essentials for practicing everything in the course from home, and I've picked options that get the job done without costing a fortune.

The short version: a flat table you already own, plastic poker cards, a cut card, a felt with padding under it, and a chip set. Add a dealer tray and spacers once you start working with a bank. That's it.

The essentials

  • A table or other flat surface. Your dining room table works fine. If you don't have one you can use, there's an inexpensive real poker table at the end of this page.
  • Plastic poker cards
  • A cut card that matches your card size
  • Felt
  • Padding to go under the felt
  • Chips, ideally a set that includes a dealer button

Cards and a cut card

You need cards made for poker, and poker cards are plastic. I advise that you do not practice with paper cards. They feel completely different, and they won't hold up to the hundreds of riffles a day you'll be doing.

Plastic cards come in two sizes: bridge size and poker size. Different casinos use different sizes, and there's no rhyme or reason to it. Bridge size is slightly narrower, so if you're practicing on a smaller surface, that's a good choice. Whatever size you get, make sure your cut card matches it. Cut cards don't come with the decks, but you can't learn the shuffle and the cut without one.

Felt and padding: don't skip the padding

The felt can be any felt. You just need enough space to practice on.

The padding is the one that people try to skip, and it's the one I'm going to insist on. Do not skip the padding. You will be miserable without it. Every poker table has foam under the felt. That give is what lets your fingers get underneath the bottom card when you shuffle and clean up the board, and underneath the chips when you cut them. Practice on a hard table without it, and the mechanics never feel right.

The setup: padding directly on your table, felt directly on top of the padding.

Chips

You'll need chips to practice just about everything: cutting, handling, buy-ins, making change, side pots. High-quality casino chips are pretty expensive. The set I recommend gets the job done for training, and it includes a dealer button and missed blind buttons. If you want to spend more on a fancier set, go for it, but it isn't necessary.

A trick for real casino chips: go to your local casino and ask for 100 of their $1 chips. It costs you $100 today, and when you're done, you bring them back and get your $100 back. That isn't enough to complete the training, because you need multiple colors, but it's a great way to practice cutting and handling the real thing.

Dealer tray, spacers and a dealer button

Once you start working with a bank, buy-ins and making change, you'll want a dealer tray. Spacers are what organize the chips in the tray, and they're sometimes used in place of missed blind buttons. You could use coins instead, but spacers are cheap and much easier.

If your chip set didn't come with a dealer button, grab one. You could use something random, but it's very inexpensive and makes practicing button movement a lot easier.

Common questions

Do I need a real poker table to learn to deal?

No. A dining room table with padding and a felt on top works for everything in my course.

Should I use bridge size or poker size cards?

Either works. Casinos use both. Bridge size is slightly narrower, which helps on a smaller practice surface. Just match your cut card to your cards.

Why do I need padding under the felt?

Every poker table has foam under the felt. That give is what lets your fingers get under the bottom card when shuffling and under the chips when cutting. Without it, the mechanics never feel right.

Can I practice with paper cards?

I don't recommend it. Poker rooms use plastic cards, and they shuffle, pitch and hold up completely differently.
