How to Deal Bomb Pots & Quarter a Pot

True Poker Dealer · Module 3, Lesson 4

A double-board bomb pot — two separate boards, each worth half the pot — is one of the more intimidating hands a new dealer can face, mostly because reading two boards and splitting an uneven pot both have to happen fast, with everyone watching and real money in the middle. Once you've got the pattern down, it's genuinely not that much harder than a normal hand. This lesson covers both the deal and the payout math.

Dealing the hand

A bomb pot replaces a normal hand at the table — everyone antes, there's no pre-flop betting round, and you deal straight to the flop. The button doesn't move for a bomb pot hand; whoever had the button for the regular game keeps it. Burn once before each street and bring both boards' cards together — one burn covers both boards, since its only job is protecting the next card from being seen, and that protection applies to both boards equally.

Counting the stub in a bomb pot

With two boards in play, there are more cards out than a normal hand — two sets of flop/turn/river plus the shared burns. Count your first eight using the burns and one board together, then continue your normal eight-counts from there against the number of players dealt in. The logic from counting the stub still applies; there's just more to account for before you start.

Reading two boards at showdown

Evaluate each board completely independently — scan the top board for the best possible hand, then do the same for the bottom board, rather than trying to hold both in your head simultaneously. Announce what you see as you go, and give players a moment to agree or correct you; double-board bomb pots are exactly where dealers make the most reading mistakes, especially early on, so there's no shame in taking the extra second to get it right instead of rushing.

Splitting a chopped or quartered pot

If one player wins a board outright and two players split the other, the sequence is: award half the pot to the outright winner first, then split what's left between the two who are chopping. Don't try to calculate everyone's share of the whole pot at once — half, then split the remainder, every time.

When a player gets quartered — they win one full board but split the other — they end up with three-quarters of the pot overall, and the player they split with gets one quarter. There's a fast way to hand this out without doing full math: give the three-quarter player their own bet back first, then split what's left (which represents the other player's bet plus the three-quarter player's remaining portion) evenly. If that remaining amount is an odd number and can't split evenly, have both players throw in one more matching chip to make it even, then split and hand back the extra — it's a shortcut, but it gets everyone the exact right amount without a single multiplication problem.

Commonly corrected in training
  • Forgetting to chop the second half of a double-board pot into quarters. This is the single most common mistake in these hands — it's easy to award the outright-won board correctly and then absentmindedly hand the other board's full amount to one player instead of splitting it.
  • Tapping the table separately for each card in a bomb pot. One tap per burn, same as a regular hand — tapping repeatedly for every card on a double board is unnecessary and slows the hand down.
  • Stacking chips so high they block a player's view of the board. It's an easy habit to fall into when there's a lot of money to organize, but a player who can't see the board can't verify what they're being paid.
  • Treating a quartered pot as one combined calculation instead of two separate halves. Combining the math can work out, but the correct procedure handles each board's payout as its own calculation — it's a cleaner habit that avoids errors when the amounts get less tidy.
  • Stacking or moving chips while a player still needs to act or see the board. Wait until the action and the reveal are genuinely finished before you start organizing the pot.
Gear for practicing this at home
  • Clay poker chips — splitting and quartering a pot cleanly takes real reps with real chips.
  • Two card decks — dealing out full double-board practice hands is the fastest way to get comfortable reading two boards at once.
  • An all-in / call button — bomb pots go all-in often; practice the two together.
