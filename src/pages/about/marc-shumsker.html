About Marc Shumsker

I'm Marc. I've worked in casinos since 2007 as a dealer, a floor supervisor and a shift manager. I've hired dealers, trained dealers, and I still deal. Everything on this site comes from that work.

How I got here

  • 2007: Blackjack dealer. I started on table games: blackjack, Ultimate Texas Hold'em, three card poker, Spanish 21, then pai gow. A few months in, I learned Texas Hold'em and Omaha, and I worked my way up from there.
  • Washington State. My first stop in the industry, in casinos in Washington.
  • South Florida: full-time floor. I worked the floor at a Seminole property and helped open the poker room at Calder Casino, a 29-table room in the grandstand of the racetrack.
  • Shift manager. At Calder I ran live dealer auditions and decided who got hired. Over my career I've done more than 1,000 live auditions where someone's job was at stake.
  • 2011–2012: True Poker Dealer. I kept hiring dealers and then having to keep training them in the back of the house. So I built a YouTube resource I could point new hires to. That became True Poker Dealer, and the shuffle lesson I recorded back then has been watched well over a million times.
  • 2020: The Complete Course. During COVID I finally finished it: a full, structured course on dealing poker.
  • Today: Remote Dealer School. I teach small live classes over Zoom, where I watch students deal and correct them in real time. I also still travel to Texas to pick up dealing shifts.

Why this site exists

Most of what you'll find online about dealing poker is written by people who've never worked in a poker room. I've been on every side of it: in the box, on the floor, and in the room running the audition. On this site I'm writing down what I actually teach, including the specific mistakes I see students make over and over in my classes. I've been logging those corrections class by class, so when I say something is the most common mistake, it's because I've counted.

The thing I tell every class

Don't burn your bridges. In this industry your value is the skill plus your relationships with the poker rooms around the country. When I was a shift manager I hired a chip runner named Victor, who wasn't even a dealer yet. Today he's the COO of Texas Card House. You never know where the people you work with are going to end up, so treat every one of them with respect.

Dealing has been one of the best things I've ever learned. Anytime I've needed it, it's been there for me, in three different states. That's what I want for my students.

Learn with me

Start with the How to Deal section of this site, or watch my lessons on YouTube. If you want structure, the Complete Course takes you through everything in order. If you want me to watch you deal and fix your mechanics before your audition, that's Remote Dealer School.
