The bank goes by a lot of names: the bank, the tray, the well, the rack. Whatever you call it, here's the thing to know. Bank skills aren't really tested in auditions, so this is the area where new dealers are consistently the weakest.

Every 20 or 30 minutes, a new dealer sits down and takes over your bank. The whole point of a standard bank setup is that they can tell what's in it in a couple of seconds, with no confusion. If you ever leave a truly messy tray behind, chances are you'll be fired.

Getting chips out of the tray

Plucking

Put your middle finger on the back of the chips, pull them up into your thumb, then put your index finger on top. It's perfect when you need just one or two chips, or a couple of one color and a couple of another, because you never have to take a stack out of the tray. Once you know where your tubes are, you can pluck without even looking.

Pinching

To take out four or five chips, use your thumb to separate them off and pick them up. With practice you can eyeball five chips. From there you can set them on the felt, or slide your thumb down, keep your index finger on top, and roll your middle finger to the side to put them straight into a splash.

Don't pull a whole stack out to count it

Pulling a stack up out of the tray to count it is how chips end up on the floor or in somebody else's stack. Instead, push the chips forward in the tube so they tilt, and pinch them back in groups. Pinch in fives for chips that cut in fives, and in fours for $25 chips. (Not sure which is which? See how to cut chips.)

Even faster: once you have one known 20-stack, you don't need to count another one. Line it up against the stack you know, see that they're the same height, and drop a spacer in.

How a standard bank is organized

  • Full stacks are 20 chips, and every full stack is capped with a clear spacer. That's casino standard. If there's no spacer on top, the next dealer should be able to assume it's not a full stack. Every completed 20-stack gets a spacer, no exceptions.
  • Largest denominations go in the middle, smallest on the outside. That's for game protection. If someone ever tried to reach in and steal a chip, they'd get the smallest denomination possible.
  • When two tubes of different denominations are the same distance from the center, the larger denomination goes on the left.
  • Each tube holds one color. Green with green, red with red. When you're forced to put two colors in one tube, put two spacers between them so surveillance can tell where one color stops.
  • One short stack per denomination. A short stack is the leftover that isn't a full 20. Keep exactly one per color, usually on the right side. Two short stacks of the same color, like 15 here and 15 there, just make everyone's life harder. Combine them.
  • Break short stacks down the way you cut them. Short stacks of $5 and $1 chips are separated into fives, and $25 chips into fours, with a spacer on each complete group. For example, a $100 short stack of greens is four chips with a spacer on top. (Personally, I don't worry about spacing the white chips at work, because you're making change with them so often. In an audition, spacing them is fine.)
  • $500 chips are the exception. Full groups are fours. If you have three left over, don't leave them as three together: put a spacer after two, and leave one on top. Two and one.

What I correct in class

A spacer on an incomplete short stack

A clear spacer on top of a short stack tells everyone it's a complete group. Five reds, five whites, four greens. Put a spacer on top of three reds, and you've told the next dealer and surveillance something that isn't true. Complete groups get a spacer. Incomplete ones don't.

One more thing: don't line all the chips up perfectly across the tray. Some bored dealers do that, and it actually makes stacks harder to verify by eye. Surveillance prefers them staggered.

Working in the tray: outside in, never cross

  • Work from the outside in. Before you touch an inner tube, use up the chips on the outside. When two tubes are equally far from the center, take from the right before the left. And always take chips off the top, never from the bottom. That's what supervisors expect.
  • The quickest fix is usually the best one. As long as you can quickly put things back in order, don't twist yourself into knots to follow the rules perfectly mid-hand. Fix your bank while the players are busy.
  • Two hands in the tray is okay, as long as they never cross. Some people teach one hand at a time. What I've always learned and taught is that two hands are fine, but crossing your hands in the tray, with one covering the other, is an opportunity for theft. Never do it.
  • Incoming chips on one side, outgoing change on the other.
  • One hand moves chips, the other moves spacers. Your strong hand handles the chips and your weak hand handles the spacers. That way you never have two hands on the same stack, and you never go hand to hand.
  • Spacers are the only thing you can hold in your palm. When you're fixing a messy bank, hold a bunch of spacers in your weak hand instead of setting them on the table. Just don't pass spacers hand to hand once chips are involved, because surveillance can't confirm nothing's hidden.
  • Clear your hands every time you come out of the tray.

What I correct in class

Crossing your arms

This is one I have to repeat constantly, across almost every class. Dealers never cross their arms or hands, whether reaching for a stack, arranging cards, or even taking notes. Crossed arms block surveillance's view and could let someone hide a chip. In an audition I was judging, crossing your arms alone would likely disqualify you. When you need to reach across, pull back and go around.

Making change: pot first, and the incoming chip goes in first

When a red chip needs to become five whites, for example to make change in the pot, put the red chip into the bank first, then give the change. Dealers who don't make that a habit constantly lose track of the chip and accidentally leave it in the pot, and then their bank is short. Put it on top of your red short stack.

And when making change for a bet, use money that's already in the pot first, before you go to the bank at all.

Cash in the well

When players buy in with cash, the cash goes into the bank. Keep it organized so the next dealer, and you, can count it fast:

  • Face it all the same direction, the way you'd display it for a fill. I teach face-down, because the number is a little bigger on the back. If the room you work in says face-up, do what they say.
  • The largest bill goes on the inside, and each smaller denomination wraps around the outside of it. That protects the bigger bills.
  • Fold the bills inward, put them in the well, and clear your hands.

If a bank started at $500 and you've got $165 in cash, you know there should be $335 in chips. That's how you check whether your bank is right.

How to do a bank fill

When you're low on chips and have built up a lot of cash, you send the cash out and get chips back. That's a fill. Lots of dealers make mistakes here, accepting the wrong amount or giving the wrong amount, and when the bank comes up wrong, they're the ones in trouble.

[[video G2EB7Ku-P3A]]

Sending the cash out

  1. Do it between hands. That's the standard time to send the money and to receive the chips, unless your room allows it during a hand.
  2. Move the deck out of the way, where it's still a little protected.
  3. Spread the cash out like a buy-in, starting on the left, and count it out loud. You need to know the amount, and so does the person taking it.
  4. Scoop it up and set it down on the table. Don't hand it to them, and don't let them reach over your bank and block the tray from surveillance.
  5. They put markers on the table showing the amount. Those stay displayed for surveillance the whole time the money is off the table. Then you keep dealing.

Receiving the chips

  1. When the chips come back, finish the hand you're on first. Then set the deck aside again.
  2. Separate the racks. Set up and splash the short stacks on your right, largest denomination first. Normally the right is your outgoing side, but during a fill nothing goes out.
  3. Set the full stacks on your left, largest denomination closest to you. You can tip the rack on its side and slide them out, or push them together from the outside. If you push them together, break them into a separate group after five 20-stacks.
  4. Break down one stack of the largest denomination, then line it up against the others and prove them all with the back of your middle fingernail. That shows they're all 20-stacks.
  5. Count it and make sure it matches the markers. Then hand back the empty racks so the person can leave.
  6. Get the chips into the tray fast: one hand picks up chips, the other hand handles spacers, and never go hand to hand. Roll stacks so they drop straight in. Clear your hands when you come out.

While a fill is on its way, it's one of the few times you want to get your short stacks ready ahead of time, so the new chips go straight in.

Inherited a disaster? If you sit down to a bank that's truly a mess, call the floor right away, so it's clear the mess wasn't yours.

Common questions about the poker bank

How many chips are in a stack in a poker chip tray?

20. Every full 20-stack is capped with a clear spacer, and the leftover chips of each color form one short stack.

Why are the highest chips in the middle of the tray?

Game protection. With the largest denominations in the center and the smallest on the outside, anyone reaching into the tray would get the lowest-value chip.

What are lammers or spacers in poker?

Small clear discs used to cap full stacks and separate groups of chips in the tray, so everyone can see the count at a glance. Rooms sometimes also use them in place of missed blind buttons.

What is a bank fill?

Sending cash from the table's bank to the cage and receiving chips back, so the game can keep going. It's done between hands, with the amount displayed for surveillance the entire time the money is off the table.

Can a dealer use two hands in the chip tray?

What I've always taught is that two hands is okay, as long as your hands never cross. Some people recommend one hand at a time. Follow your room's rule.
