Every rule about handling chips comes back to one idea. Cameras watch all casino activity, to protect the casino, the players and the employees. One of the things surveillance is watching for is a chip disappearing into a palm, then a sleeve or a pocket. Good chip handling makes it obvious, all the time, that there's nothing in your hands that shouldn't be.

That protects you too. As a dealer, you want it to be impossible for anyone to even wonder.

Clearing your hands

Clearing your hands means quickly showing your palms and the backs of your hands, palm to the ceiling, then the back of your hand to the ceiling. It can be one fast motion. You do it:

  • After you push a pot. Every single time, until it's automatic.
  • After you come out of the chip tray.
  • Before and after the cut, so everyone can see your hands are empty.
  • When you go from one stack to another while stacking chips.
  • When you put a chip down to switch hands.
  • Before and after you touch your face, hair or clothes, and before and after you reach below the rail.

What I correct in class

Forgetting to clear after the pot

I tell almost every class the same thing: build the habit of clearing your hands right after you push a pot, every time, with no exceptions, so you never have to think about it. Scratching your nose or fixing your hair mid-hand counts too. Clear before and after.

Fingertips, never palms

Hold a single chip in your fingertips. Hold a stack of chips in your fingertips: thumb on one side, and your forefinger, middle finger and ring finger touching the felt, with full control of the chips. There should always be room in your palm. When chips stay in your fingertips, they can't end up in your palm, and nobody can wonder if they did.

The same goes for dragging chips in. Slide them with your fingertips. Don't scoop them into your palm or grab them in your fist.

What I correct in class

Grabbing chips in your hand

I stop students when they pick chips up in a way that looks like palming, even when they obviously aren't. It matters most with high-denomination chips. If it looks like palming to the camera, it's a problem. Keep the chips in your fingertips and slide them.

Never go hand to hand

Passing a chip or a stack straight from one hand to the other is called going hand to hand, and you don't do it as a dealer. It's another opportunity for theft, and surveillance can't confirm nothing was kept.

Instead: put the chip or stack down on the felt in front of you, clear your hand, and pick it up with your other hand. It's a tiny bit slower, and once you're used to it, you won't notice the difference.

A lot of dealers who start out in underground games never learn this, and it's hard to fix later. In the bank, this goes for spacers too. Passing spacers hand to hand while you're working with chips means surveillance can't confirm nothing's hidden.

Getting a stack into the tray

This is where dealers go hand to hand without realizing it. They pick up a stack, find their fingers are in the way of the tray slot, and shuffle the chips into the other hand. Instead, use one of these:

  • The roll. Thumb on top, forefinger, middle finger or both on the bottom, and roll the stack toward yourself. Now it drops right into the slot. Make sure your grip is firm, or the chips spill.
  • The fingernail grip. Index finger fingernail-down on top of the stack, thumb on one side and middle finger on the other. Then drop it into the tray.

Two more tray habits: never cross your hands in the tray, with one hand covering the other, and keep incoming chips on one side and outgoing change on the other.

Handing a stack to a player

When a stack is finished, like after a buy-in, hand it off with that fingernail grip: index finger nail-down on top, thumb and middle finger on the sides, ring finger if you need it. It looks professional, the cameras can see it clearly, and you can send the stack further than if you just picked it up and held it out.

Pushing a pot

When the whole pot goes to one player:

  • Surround the chips with both hands and push them all to the player, even if it's a lot of chips. Don't pick the chips up and set them back down, and don't stack them up and hand them over.
  • Push it all the way to the player.
  • If the pot is spread out, it's fine to pull the chips together a little first. That's called tightening the pot. If there's too much to push at once, push part of it, then go back and push the rest. Lots of dealers don't realize you can push a pot in two motions.
  • Clear your hands as you come away.

What I correct in class

A shaky push

A pot push that looks hesitant hurts the "feels like poker" test. Push with confidence. Also watch for bumping into other players' stacks. If the pot has to travel through other chips, separate them first so nothing mixes.

What happens right after the push: push pot, move button, drop rake. Do it in that order, every time, immediately. Kill the losing hand before you push, and keep the board intact until the pot is pushed in case there's a dispute. I personally struggled for about two years with forgetting where the button was, because I never built the habit of moving it right after the push. Build it from day one. And every time you touch the pot to push it, check that you took the rake.

Stacking chips for a split pot

When two players have the same hand and split the pot, the only way to know how much each gets is to stack it up. Do it with your fingertips. Pick up chips in your fingertips, keep them out of your palm, set the stack down when it's tall enough, clear your hand, and start the next stack.

You'll sometimes see dealers slide chips back into their hand with their thumb and forefinger. That's for non-value chips, in roulette or tournaments. In a cash game, every chip is real money, and if anyone ever tells you to do it that way in poker, they're confusing the two.

Never play with gaming equipment

When a player is taking a long time to decide, which is normal in a no-limit cash game, you'll sometimes see a dealer riffle chips, spin the dealer button, or fiddle with spacers. Don't. As a player, you'd find it unprofessional, you'd be less comfortable with that dealer at your table, and you'd wonder if they were up to something.

Common questions about handling chips

What does "clearing your hands" mean?

Showing the palms and backs of your hands to the camera, usually in one quick motion, to prove you aren't holding anything. Dealers do it after pushing a pot, after the tray, around the cut, and any time they touch their face or clothes.

Why can't dealers pass chips from one hand to the other?

Going hand to hand is an opportunity to palm a chip, and surveillance can't confirm nothing was kept. Set the chip down, clear your hand, and pick it up with the other hand.

What order does a dealer do things after a hand?

Push the pot, move the button, drop the rake, in that order, right away.

How do you split a pot as a dealer?

Stack the chips with your fingertips to see how much is there, then divide it into equal stacks.
