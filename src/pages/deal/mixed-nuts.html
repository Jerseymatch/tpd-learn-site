How to practice with it

Mixed Nuts shows you practice boards with several hands, and you pick the winning hand. It shows you your mistakes, so you learn the hand you misread instead of just getting a wrong answer.

  • Start with hold'em. Get to where you can read every hand with zero errors before you worry about speed.
  • Then work on speed. Time yourself on 10 hands. Accuracy first, always. A fast wrong read is worse than a slow right one.
  • Move to the mixed games. It covers the flop games (hold'em, Omaha 8, Big O 8), the stud games (7-stud 8 and razz) and the draw games (2-7 triple draw, Badugi and Baducey). Badugi is hard, because you have to count suits.

For a benchmark, here are my own times for 10 hands with zero errors: hold'em 1:08.97, 2-7 triple draw 1:48.80, Omaha 8 2:39.52, Badugi 3:12.79 and Big O 3:17.72.

The app is harder than a real table. At a real table you aren't reading five hands every time, and players protect their own hands. So if Mixed Nuts feels hard, that's the point. If you can read it here, you can read it at the table.

Where it fits in your practice

Use Mixed Nuts alongside the rest of your home practice. Learn the hand rankings and how I read hands at showdown first, then use Mixed Nuts to build speed. For split pots, learn the procedure on the table, because the game won't teach you how to push the chips.
