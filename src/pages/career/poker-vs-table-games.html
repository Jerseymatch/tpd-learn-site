I started working in casinos in 2007 as a blackjack dealer. I learned blackjack, Spanish 21, three card poker and Ultimate Texas Hold'em first, then pai gow, and within a few months I learned Texas Hold'em and Omaha and worked my way up from there. I've also dealt a little craps. So I've been on both sides of this question, and I've taught table games dealers in my classes who wanted to switch to poker.

They're both dealing jobs, and a lot of the skills carry over. But they're built on a completely different relationship between you, the players and the house, and that changes almost everything about how you act at the table.

The big difference: you can't root for anyone

In table games, the players play against the house, and the math takes care of the house. So as a dealer you can cheer for the player. It's better for them, and you get tipped more. When I dealt table games, I was trained to make the game fun and get excited when players won.

In poker, the players play against each other. Every pot has a winner and a loser at the same table, so the dealer never takes sides. That was the biggest adjustment for me going from blackjack to poker. Every positive interaction you have with players has to dance around that.

It also changes what a mistake means. If a table games dealer makes a mistake in a player's favor, the house takes the hit. In poker, a mistake that helps one player hurts another player at the table. There are more chances for mistakes in a hand of poker than in most table games, too, which is why knowing exactly what to do in every situation is the number one skill for a poker dealer.

What I correct in class

"Good luck" and "nice hand"

Students who've dealt table games ask about this, and some dealers bring the habit with them. In poker, we never say good luck to anybody, and if someone's all in, we don't say "good luck, all in." If you say good luck to one person, you're basically saying bad luck to everyone else. You'll hear it from dealers who crossed over from table games without really getting the difference, and players hear it and think that's good service. It isn't, in poker. Talking to players goes deeper on staying neutral.

Mechanics: table games dealers are ahead

Table games mechanics are cleaner. It's the house's money on the table, surveillance watches closely, and the procedures have to be precise. Poker rooms accept worse mechanics. The mechanics I teach for poker are really table games mechanics. My chip cutting is table games style, and my shuffle came from table games.

So dealers who start in table games tend to have smoother hands, and they usually fly through the first two weeks of my course, which are mostly mechanics. Bank maintenance comes easily to them too. I support starting straight in poker, but table games also teach you guest service and how to handle problem players.

Some things are different, and they're the habits I work on with table games dealers in class:

  • Which way the cards face. In table games, you don't want players to see the cards, because if they can track a card, that costs the house. In poker there's no house money, so it's the opposite: the dealer doesn't want to see the cards. The players don't want you to know which card is which. In poker, the deck always faces away from you.
  • The wash and the shuffle. Some of the card motions go the other direction in table games, and a dealer who only knows a table games shuffle has to learn the poker sequence: riffle, riffle, box, riffle, cut. See how to shuffle cards.
  • The bank. In table games, the chip tray shows how much money the table has made today, so the supervisor tracks the large chips going in and out all day. In poker, the bank is an impress: a set amount that stays the same. Working the poker bank covers it.
  • Apologizing. Table games trains dealers never to apologize. In poker you still don't apologize when someone loses a hand, because that's part of the game. But if you say a bet is 150 and it was 100, a quick "my bad, I thought it was 150, it's 100" is totally fine.

Talking to players: a fine line in poker

Table games are more conversational. A table games dealer has a lot more to do with whether a player stays at the table. In poker, a player sits down with a table full of other players. Whether they win or lose, it's their choice to stay or go, and the dealer has much less to do with it.

In poker, talking can influence the game, and players are concentrating. If you can engage people at the right moments without affecting the game, it takes a lot of knowledge and experience, and you'll do great with tips. But if it affects the game in my room, I'm going to be upset. A lot of table games dealers come to poker looking like superstars and then have to unlearn the talking.

Tournaments are the extreme. Tournament dealers aren't tipped hand by hand, so chatting has almost no benefit. The good tournament dealers are very quiet and just do their job very well.

When something goes wrong

A table games dealer handles more of the small stuff before calling a supervisor over. In poker, the players are trying to beat each other, and a confrontation at a poker table tends to spread. So a poker dealer never argues with a player. If you get resistance, you call the floor, and the floor handles it. And where table games sometimes has a bit more flexibility to give the player what's fair, in poker giving one player something can take it from another, so the rules set out in advance decide. More on that in when to call the floor.

Tips and pay

In the US, poker dealers usually earn more than table games dealers, with exceptions. It depends on where you are. Table games rooms often pool tips. In poker, most rooms let you keep your own, so the faster and better you deal, the more you make. A dead table is harder on a poker dealer who keeps their own tips than on a blackjack dealer whose tips are pooled, that's true. But because you keep your own, connecting with a player on a dead spread can pay off later.

Thanking players works differently too. In poker, tips are close to automatic, but skipping the thank-you gives a reluctant tipper a reason to stop. Say thank you every time, but act cool about it. Over-thanking in poker actually discourages tips. Poker dealer pay and tips covers the money side, and tipping the dealer covers it from the player's side.

Which job is better?

It depends on what you want. Here's why I chose poker.

  • Poker stays interesting. Three card poker without the side bets got boring for me within minutes. In poker, you get to sit and watch real decisions in a game of skill, with new dynamics every day.
  • You sit down. Poker dealers sit. Most table games dealers stand.
  • The money. In the US, poker usually pays dealers better.

Table games have their own appeal. They're more social, you get to root for people, and a great craps crew is something special: regulars love playing with them. Craps is the most complicated game in the casino to deal. I got good at it, never great.

Keep in mind that poker is a small part of a casino's business. In my estimate, slots bring in around 70% of a casino's revenue, table games around 20-something percent, and poker around 5%.

Switching from table games to poker

If you already deal table games, you're in great shape. A table games dealer can usually skip the first two weeks of mechanics and get the poker rules and procedures down in about three to four weeks of course work plus table practice. What you'll spend your time on:

  • The rules and procedures. Betting rules, side pots, the showdown, misdeals. Start with how to deal a hand of Texas Hold'em.
  • The pitch. It's different from table games. See how to pitch cards.
  • The mindset. Stay neutral, follow the action, and let the floor handle disputes.

Going the other way works too. My mechanics transferred from table games to poker and back without trouble. The mentality is what you have to switch.

Common questions about poker vs table games dealing

Do poker dealers make more than blackjack dealers?

In the US, usually yes, with exceptions. It depends on where you are. Poker dealers usually keep their own tips, while table games tips are often pooled.

Is it easier to deal poker or blackjack?

Table games mechanics are stricter, but poker has more rules and more chances for mistakes in a hand. In my view, learning poker with good mechanics is in the same ballpark as learning craps.

Can a blackjack dealer become a poker dealer?

Yes, and they start ahead. A table games dealer can usually skip the mechanics and learn the poker rules and procedures in about three to four weeks plus practice.

Why can't poker dealers root for players?

Because the players play each other. Rooting for one player is rooting against another player at the same table.

Do most poker dealers start in table games?

No, but the ones who do tend to have smoother mechanics, because table games procedures are more precise.

Is Ultimate Texas Hold'em poker?

No. It's a table game. The player plays against the dealer, with an ante, a blind and an optional trips bet. In some places, poker dealers also deal poker-based table games like three card poker and pai gow.

What's the hardest casino game to deal?

Craps. So much happens so fast, with a lot of memorization.
