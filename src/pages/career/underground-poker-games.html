What casinos see in underground dealers

In an underground game, nobody corrects you. You build habits, and nobody ever tells you they're wrong. A casino is different. Casinos need procedures that surveillance can follow. That's what an audition is checking. The underground habits I see most

Here are habits I've corrected that come from home and underground games:

  • Cutting chips with two hands. A casino dealer cuts with one. See how to cut chips.
  • Passing chips from hand to hand. Say a player tosses you a tip that lands way over on your left. A lot of dealers, especially ones who started underground, reach for it and pass it from one hand to the other. Many never learn not to, and it's so hard to undo later. How to handle chips shows the right way.
  • A bent index finger on the shuffle. Anyone who has played a lot of home-game poker tends to bend the index finger on top of the deck during the riffle, because that's what they've seen other people do. Keep it flat. You get more control and much less tension in your hands. How to shuffle cards walks through the grip.
  • Holding the deck after the wash. In a home game people pick up the cards and just hold them. In a casino, as soon as you're done washing, you put the deck down flat and go straight into your shuffle. There's no time where you're holding it.

What I correct in class

When a student has played a lot of home games, they almost always have the bent index finger on the riffle. It's not a talent problem. It's an instinct from watching other people shuffle. We flatten the finger and put the thumbs on the corners so they take over control. The same goes for the deck that hangs in the air after the wash: put it down and go.
